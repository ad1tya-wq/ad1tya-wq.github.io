### Feasibility: a realistic "power" mechanism (not implemented, as asked)
- The drag already is a power mechanism: drag length sets |v| linearly, drag direction sets the heading, and the readout expresses it as a multiple of the local escape speed √(2GM/(r−r_s)), so 1.0× is the physically meaningful threshold (below: bound, will fall in or orbit; above: leaves, unless it passes inside ~2 r_s where nothing helps).
- If you want an explicit control, three options in rising realism:
  1. **Charge bar (hold to charge).** Hold the launch button, a bar fills from 0 to 2× escape speed, release fires along the current aim. Cheap (≈40 lines), familiar, works on touch and keyboard (hold Space). Recommended if you want a "power" feel.
  2. **Δv budget.** Give the probe a fixed propellant budget (say 1.4× escape) and let the visitor split it between launch speed and a single mid-course burn triggered by a tap. Teaches the Oberth effect for free (burning near periapsis buys more). Medium (≈120 lines, needs a burn UI and a second preview).
  3. **Thrust profile.** Continuous thrust while a key is held, with mass depletion (rocket equation) shown in the readout. Most realistic, but it turns the toy into a game and the status line into a HUD; I would not do this on a portfolio.
- None of them needs shader work; all run through the existing `predict()`/`setProbe` path. My pick: option 1 if you want more control, otherwise leave the drag as is; it is already the physical model.

## 3. Branch `feature/devour`: the mass slider game (first working build, `61ff923`)
Branched from main after the wave and slingshot fixes, so it contains both.

What is in it:
- Horizon now has two controls under the hole: Launch (left) and **Mass** (right; a native range slider, hairline track, core thumb, readout `1.0 → 10.0 M☉`, `aria-valuetext` in solar masses).
- Mass scales the hole 1× → 2.5× (shadow, photon ring, lensing, disk radius) and the slingshot gravity (`massScale()` in `lifecycle.ts`; the toy's `gm` uses it, so probes get captured more easily at high mass).
- Reach: `reach(mass)` grows as `((m − 0.04)/0.96)^1.5 × farthest`, so nothing happens in the first 4 % and everything is within reach at 1. Blocks (every leaf text element in `main`, the portrait, then the contact details, then the topbar) are ordered by document distance from the hole and eaten in that order; hysteresis of 24 px stops flicker at a threshold. 112 blocks on desktop, 114 on a phone (the readout is hidden there; the inline project details are not).
- Eating a block: its glyphs are sampled with the hero-name sampler, the DOM copy goes `visibility: hidden` in place (no reflow, no scroll jump), and the particles fly a decaying orbit into the disk over 1.6 s (radius eases in, the spin rate rises as it gets closer, a golden-ratio lag smears each word into a streak, then the particles hand over to the Keplerian disk and stay there). Frames in the harness show spiral arms of text wrapping into the disk (`dv-250-1000.png`).
- Preserve across scroll: yes. Scrolling back up after feeding shows sections with only their hairlines left.
- Reversible: lowering the slider restores blocks in reverse; particles fly the orbit backwards out of the disk to the glyphs, then the DOM copy reappears. A block interrupted mid-flight reverses from where it is (no jump).
- Void: at mass 1 with everything gone the status reads "Nothing escapes." and `Reset the universe?` appears after the last flight; it sets `history.scrollRestoration = 'manual'` and reloads, landing at the top with the name re-assembling. Verified: after reset scrollY 0, nothing eaten, slider at 0.
- The hero name and portrait, when eaten, also hide their resting particles (`uEatenNameFace`), so the hero does not silently rebuild itself on scroll-up.
- Reduced motion: hole scales, blocks fade (400 ms) instead of flying, reset appears after the fade. Verified 83 blocks eaten at 0.7 under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Particle pool: the last 20 % of the budget (≈10k on desktop, 4k on phones), ring-allocated; a block gets area/8 points (60–3000). When the ring wraps the oldest block loses its slots (its particles are already in the disk, indistinguishable); if such a block is later restored it fades back in instead of flying. Fine for the effect, noted here as a known simplification.
- Budget: 63.5 KB gz (+2.7 KB). 44 unit tests (ordering, reach, hysteresis, points, ring pool, predict, escape speed). Impeccable detector: 0 findings.
